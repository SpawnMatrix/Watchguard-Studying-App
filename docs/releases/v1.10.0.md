# Version 1.10.0 — Hands-on labs with a simulated Firebox

Labs had instructions and checkpoint questions, but nothing to actually configure. Most learners have no Firebox, so "add a Host IPv4 route" stayed words on a page. Six labs now open a simulated Firebox and management PC beside the steps. You do the work, and the lab checks it.

## What a hands-on lab looks like

Open Lab 1, 3, 6, 9, 10 or 17 (marked **hands-on** in the catalogue). The catalogue steps aside, and the step instructions sit next to a simulated Firebox:

- **A lab bench:** power and cabling, plus a management PC that can run `ipconfig`, `ping`, open a web page, and run a speed test.
- **A Fireware-style Web UI:**
  - Front Panel, Traffic Monitor and FireWatch
  - System Status > Routes
  - Network > Interfaces and Routes
  - Firewall Policies, Policy Checker and Traffic Management
  - Global Settings, Users and Roles, Configuration File, Backup and Restore Image, and Upgrade OS
  - on a factory-default Firebox, a setup wizard
- **A task on each step.** For example: "edit interface 2: name DMZ, type Optional, 192.168.10.1/24". The task shows ✓ Verified when the simulated configuration, and what you ran, match. A step now needs both its task and its checkpoint question before it completes, so a lab can't be clicked through.

## Traffic behaves the way the lab expects

Traffic from the management PC goes through the Firebox's route table and its policies, and each connection is written to Traffic Monitor in Fireware's log format. So the lessons show up the way they would on a real device:

- **Lab 6 (Routing):** after the host route, a ping to 8.8.4.4 leaves through DMZ and times out, and Traffic Monitor shows the change. Adding the route before the DMZ interface exists is refused, with a message explaining why.
- **Lab 10 (Packet Filters):** an `*.example.com` deny sorts above the HTTP-proxy policy, so example.com is blocked while other sites still load. Restricting DNS to a server the clients don't use breaks name resolution.
- **Lab 9 (Traffic Management):** the speed test drops to 1 Mbps down and 0.5 Mbps up, but only once the global Traffic Management setting is on.
- **Lab 3 (Backup and Restore):** restoring the automatic pre-upgrade image is refused because it was made for Fireware 12.9.2, which is the point of that lab.

"Reset simulator to this step" puts the device back to where the step begins. The simulator isn't saved; reopening a lab rebuilds it from your step progress.

Labs that need a second Firebox, Active Directory, Dimension or WatchGuard Cloud keep their instructions and checkpoints.

This is a teaching simulation of the behaviour these labs exercise, not Fireware. Policy order is a simplified automatic order: specific destinations before zones, specific services before Outgoing, Denied before Allowed.

## Verification

- For each of the six labs, every task is unmet at the start of its step and verified after doing exactly what it asks.
- Tests cover routing, policy order, DNS restriction, traffic management, backup version checks and passphrase rules. They also check that the simulator never modifies the state it was given.
- Every simulator page renders, including locked pages on a factory-default Firebox.
- Labs 1, 6 and 10 were played through with the real controls in the browser. There's no overflow at 390px, where the simulator menu becomes a dropdown, and light-theme text is 5:1 contrast or better.

No server, database or deployment change is required.
