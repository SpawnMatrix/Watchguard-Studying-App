# Version 1.26.0 — Tell it your exam date and get a plan for today

Exam readiness (1.15.0) tells a learner where they stand in each exam
category. It does not say what to do with the days left, and that is the
question most people bring to a study tool once they have booked an exam.

## Exam plan

A new card at the top of **Progress & Admin** asks for the exam date, stored
per learning track, and turns it into:

- **A countdown and a phase.** More than 14 days out is the learning phase,
  4–14 days is revision, the last three days are for final review, then exam
  day. Each phase gets one sentence of advice.
- **A daily question target**, split across categories, with the reason for
  each: "5 more answers before it can be scored", "at 60%, below the 85%
  target", "10 missed questions to re-answer", or "N unseen questions to
  cover".
- **Mock exam dates.** Weekly while learning, every three days while
  revising, and a last full mock two days before the exam, leaving a day to
  act on it. Inside three days there is no mock, because there is no time to
  act on one.

Study Home shows "Exam in N days · see today's plan", linking to the card.

## The arithmetic, which is meant to be checkable

`src/engine/examPlan.ts` is plain arithmetic over the readiness rows that
`examReadiness()` already produces. There is no model.

For each category, the work left is:

- the larger of the answers still needed before it can be scored
  (`READINESS_MIN_ANSWERS`, 5) and the unseen questions needed to have answered
  60% of its bank,
- **plus** every question answered wrong the last time. Re-answering those is
  the cheapest gain there is, and the work does not vanish just because a
  category is covered.

The total is spread over the days before the exam. The eve of the exam is not
counted, because it is for a light review. The daily figure is clamped between
10, a warm-up, and 80. When even 80 a day would not cover the work, the card
says so and tells the learner to favour the top of the list. The day's
questions go to categories in proportion to work left × exam weight, by
largest remainder, so the parts always add up to the target. A heavier
category gets more of the day for the same amount of work.

The target is **85%**, the practice target the Training Analytics panel
already uses, not the NSE pass mark of 75%. A plan that aims at the pass mark
leaves no room for a bad day.

Days are counted in **local calendar dates**: 0 on exam day, negative
afterwards, and correct across daylight-saving changes (tested across 25
October and 1 November). The exam date is entered and stored as a calendar
date, not a timestamp, so it means the same day in every time zone.

## Kept out of the first bundle

`examPlan.ts` imports only a type. Study Home needs only the date and a day
count, and pulling in readiness would have put the whole question catalogue
into the bundle every visitor downloads first. A test fails if the module ever
gains a runtime import.

## Storage

A new study key, **`watchguard-exam-date-v1`**, holds dates per track, for
example `{"local":"2026-10-29"}`. It syncs with the account. The server
accepts only known tracks and real calendar dates, so `2026-02-31` and
`next tuesday` are both rejected. `docs/privacy.md` lists it, and the
inventory test from 1.24.0 enforced that. An exam date is a new kind of fact
about a person, so the entry says plainly what it is.

## Verification

- `npm run lint` clean. `npx vitest run`: 54 files, **463 tests**, green.
  - Engine: day counting across DST, date validation, spreading work over study
    days, the 10/80 clamps and the "behind" flag, missed questions counted as
    work, weight-proportional splitting that sums exactly, the wording of
    reasons, mock scheduling in each phase, stopping on exam day, both real
    blueprints, and the import guard.
  - Render: the card asks for a date when there is none, renders a 30-day plan
    with phase, split and mock dates, and ignores a malformed saved value.
- Driven in a production build: entered a date 24 days out through the form.
  The card showed "24 days to go", 13 questions a day split 4/4/2/1/1/1 across
  the six NSE categories, and mock exams on the 12th and the 27th. Study Home
  showed "Exam in 24 days · see today's plan". The pill first sat inline before
  the main button; it now has its own line, checked by measuring the layout.
